# The REAL Living Lab — ENoLL Application Backbone

A reusable mapping of the Lab against the European Network of Living Labs criteria. Use this to complete the free ENoLL self-assessment and as the spine of a formal application. Status sought at entry: **Accepted to Grow Member**.

## What the Lab is, in one paragraph

The REAL Living Lab is the citizen-facing home of REAL Analytics, a research programme at Atlantic Technological University, Galway. It runs across a network of three real-world teaching sites built on one repeatable design: Gairdín DANÚ (a community garden and registered charity), the ATU greenhouse (the academic site), and a template greenhouse (the proving node, built and documented as an open blueprint so any site can replicate it and join). Each site streams the same open environmental data, water, humidity, light and soil, and hosts citizen science, creative participation, and learning analytics, all built on one practice: radical kindness toward self, others and the living world. The guiding question across every setting is whether the work makes someone feel capable.

## The five core elements

- **Active user involvement.** Children, students, families and community members gather data, make sightings, and shape sessions across the three sites.
- **Real-life setting.** Three settings in genuine daily use: a community garden, a university greenhouse, and a replicable template.
- **Multi-stakeholder participation.** A full Quadruple Helix (see Stability below).
- **Multi-method approach.** Environmental sensing, citizen science, place-based creative participation, and learning analytics.
- **Co-creation.** Students and community help decide what is grown, what is measured, and what is asked, not only read a dashboard.

## The six building blocks (Harmonised Evaluation Framework)

**Strategy.** One enduring question, does this make someone feel capable. A three-site network on one repeatable design, where replicability is the sustainability model. Growth happens by others copying the template, not by central expansion alone.

**Operations.** A genuine multi-method approach: environmental sensing (water, humidity, light, soil), citizen science badges, creative participation, and learning analytics. Governance sits with ATU and the DANÚ charity. Consent, safeguarding and a simple open-data protocol are defined, with particular care for work involving children.

**Openness.** Open-source code; an open live environmental dataset published per site; an open hardware-and-method blueprint for the template greenhouse. Privacy by architecture: no tracking, no cookies, no accounts for public tools. GDPR by design.

**Users and Reality.** Three real settings in daily use. Co-creation is built in from the outset: at each site the community and students help choose what is measured and studied, which is the line that separates a living lab from a sensor project.

**Impact and Value.** Longitudinal open datasets from day one; European recognition through EU GREEN (winning image, EU GREEN Illuminating Discovery); the co-built willow sensory enclosure as a lasting legacy; engagement evidence from the badge work; outcomes gathered with the CreaTures evaluation framework. The open dataset is itself a public good.

**Stability and Collaboration.** A full Quadruple Helix: academia (ATU, REAL Analytics, Dr Etain Kiely, Dr Yvonne Lang, Dr Mossy Kelly and his students in the ATU Physics Department engineering the sensor rigs); community (Gairdín DANÚ, This Is Me Initiative Donegal, Men's Shed); public and government (Creative Ireland via Galway County Council, CLÁR, NPWS and Biodiversity Office, the HEA, the EU GREEN alliance); and creative practice (artists Emma Donoghue and Emer McDermott, occupational therapy, the evaluation partner Amicitia). Backed institutionally by ATU, with the template as the internal scaling story and ENoLL and EU GREEN as the external links.
